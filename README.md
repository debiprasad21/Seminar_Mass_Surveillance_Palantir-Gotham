# Palantir Gotham and the Attributes of Contemporary Mass Surveillance

SoftLang Seminar, Universität Koblenz, 2026
Debiprasad Dash

**RQ.** How do the capabilities of Palantir Gotham correspond to three core
attributes of contemporary mass surveillance — bulk data collection,
integration of heterogeneous data sources, and long-term storage for
retrospective analysis — as described in the literature?

## Method
Semi-systematic / narrative review (Snyder 2019). Three seed papers supplied with the topic, plus citation-graph snowballing and keyword search. 
Search and selection reported per van Wee & Banister (2024); synthesis as a correspondence matrix per Ramdhani et al. (2014) 
Final corpus: 7 papers.

## Finding
Gotham corresponds strongly to heterogeneous/third-party integration and to storage for retrospective analysis, and only partially to bulk collection i.e Does not collect; it integrates and makes searchable bulk data others collected.

## How to validate a finding

Open the presentation in `slides/` → **slide 7** (*Analysis & Data Extracted*). Each row gives a paper, what was extracted, and the exact phrase and page.

1. Pick a row and note the phrase and page.
2. Open that paper by its DOI in `corpus/references.md`.
3. Text-search the phrase (Ctrl+F) on the stated page — it appears verbatim.
4. Check it supports the correspondence claimed on slides 9 & 10
   
## How to reproduce the search
1. The search terms listed in `artifacts/searchlog.md` can be re-run on Google Scholar.
2. Snowballing:"ontology Gotham" in Scholar → Galis & Karlsson 2024 (top hit) → its Connected Papers graph → Iliadis & Acker 2022 , Ulbricht & Egbert 2024
3. The screenshots stored in `artifacts/` record the result lists observed in April–May 2026 for those searches.

## Limitation
Connected Papers rebuilds its graph as new work is indexed, so the neighbourhood may differ from the screenshots. The seed paper (Galis & Karlsson 2024) and the date of graph generation are recorded so the selection chain stays auditable.
